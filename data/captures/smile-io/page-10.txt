Three pressures.
Every apparel brand manages them at once.

01

Acquisition costs keep climbing

Paid social is a treadmill. Every increase in acquisition cost eats product margin, so you acquire more customers just to stay flat. Referrals turn the customers you already have into the cheapest channel you own.

02

Discounting is a race to the bottom

Matching every competitor's 20% off devalues the brand while teaching your audience to never pay full price. Points are a brand-safe alternative: an earned reward instead of a blanket markdown.

03

Seasons create revenue whiplash

Fashion is driven by newness, so the weeks between collections hurt, and the post-holiday slump hurts most. A program gives you a direct line to your best customers, with bonus point events and early access to fill the quiet stretches.

What to reward.
And what to give back.

Points for reviews

Social proof carries the weight that a fitting room used to. Reward detailed reviews, and reward photo reviews more, so future shoppers can see fit on a real body.

Points for social engagement

A follow or a tagged post is a customer publicly aligning their identity with your brand. That is worth more than the impression it earns, so reward it.

Exclusive access as a reward

Early access to the new collection, or a member-only drop, costs nothing in margin and creates real urgency. In fashion, status routinely outperforms a discount.

Tiers built on status, not just multipliers

Free tailoring, event invites, anniversary gifts, and a dedicated service line make the top tier a place worth staying, which is what separates a club from a punch card.

Fixed-dollar rewards, not percentages

A $10 reward is easier to understand than 10% off and protects margin on your higher-priced pieces. Free shipping is the easiest first reward to earn.

One balance across retail and online

Apparel customers move between the store and the site without thinking about it. Points earned in one place have to be spendable in the other.
